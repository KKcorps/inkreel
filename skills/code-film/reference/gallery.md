# The gallery

Read this before you write the intent card, and again before you show anything.
It holds 29 films that people loved, where Claude drew every frame in code, and
what they share. It shows the taste. It is not a set of templates.

## How to use this file

Pick the two or three films nearest your intent and watch them whole. Take
what they mean, not how they look. Many makers borrowed the fly's navy
blueprint plates, and by late September that look was already a cliché. A
piece that could sit next to these films without looking like a copy of one
is the goal.

## Ours

- **Parcel 42's day, approved.** 37 s, a Pinot upsert explainer. The first
  reviewer called it perfect. The source is in `assets/examples/parcel42-day/`.
  Riso scenes of one parcel's day, a kid who asks where it is, a snail with a
  late update, cut against a clerk's ledger whose album of postcards fades
  exactly when rows are struck. It works for four reasons: a composed tune, not
  a sound per motion; a world you could live in cut against the diagram of its
  insides; one character with a face acting in every scene; and the album that
  makes the day and the diagram the same picture. Its first words were a
  record, a question, rows and labels, and read like separate phrases, so the
  third version rewrote them as one voice: the kid asks, the parcel answers,
  a refrain ("the old one stays, just switched off."), a pattern the snail breaks. Read it for the method, not the look.
- **Parcel 42, the switch, approved.** 130 s, a narrated lesson on how Pinot
  upserts hide old rows, made with the `lesson-film` skill, which holds its
  source as that skill's worked example. Kartik called v8 "perfect". It works
  because the voice sets the clock and every picture lands on its word, the
  script is one conversation that hands the viewer each term before using it,
  one town carries the whole mechanism (houses are rows, streets are
  segments, a lit window is a bit that is on), and it opens and ends the way
  Kurzgesagt does: a human first line, a title sting after the question, and a
  slow outro that rises back to the sky it started from.
- **The ledger, rejected.** 29 s, the same topic on one ledger page with a
  camera moving over it and a pluck on every pen stroke. "Just beats on every
  motion", and not on par with these films. It passed every check.
- **every-row-twice, rejected.** See the section below.

## What the loved films share

These are judgements, not rules. Each one names the films that show it. A
piece can break one when its intent card says why.

- **There is a thing to follow.** A girl and a smiling star, a fly, a drop, a
  request, a molecule, an ink star, an orange box called Clawd. The viewer
  tracks one thing through every scene, so twenty pictures read as one story.
  The weakest middles in the gallery are lists with nothing to follow.
- **Every picture says something.** In music films the picture proves the
  lyric, often as a pun. In explainers the picture is an everyday thing and
  the label is the real term: a vine tagged `<script>`, stirred tea for
  Navier-Stokes. In the best ones the words become the picture. Phrases pile
  into a loaf and a cat in small print. "warm water" draws the ocean in the
  typhoon.
- **It ends where it began, changed.** The night town, the letter on the desk,
  the terminal prompt, the stage curtain, the raindrop, the morning request.
  About two thirds of the gallery bookends or loops.
- **Something shows where we are.** A P(doom) thermometer climbs, a sample
  counter runs, a journey log tracks altitude, a wind gauge peaks and falls, a
  year stamp ticks, an alarm clock runs out. It gives the middle a direction.
- **The look is a real material, used with its flaws.** Paper collage with torn
  edges, woodcut ink, watercolour washes, riso with misregistration, sand,
  glass tiles, a hard pixel grid. The material is often the idea: the tiles
  were never glued down, the sand wipe is the cut. Riso is one look among many.
- **Sound and picture are one piece.** Either the sounds land on the picture's
  moments, or the picture moves with the music's phrases. When the music comes
  from the same code, cuts can sit on bar lines. The sound drops out just
  before the big moment and hits on the cut. A silent film is a real choice,
  because X autoplays muted.
- **Text is rare, big and part of the world.** A note held up, one word under a
  picture, a painted sound word, lyrics in two sizes (small subtitles for
  verses, giant type for the hook). Text names things. It never explains the
  picture. Tiny text is one of the most common weaknesses.
- **It has a point of view and some warmth.** Many are about Claude itself:
  its eyes, its feed at 3 am, its own music, a band drawn from the code that
  plays it. The joke or the feeling could only come from this maker about
  this subject.
- **It is as short as the idea.** The most loved films run 20 to 40 seconds.
  The long ones (4 to 11 minutes) sag into slide-deck layouts, even when the
  content is good.
- **Explainers start from the question.** "A typhoon isn't wind." They show the
  thing break before naming the fix, and the last frame is a summary a
  newcomer could repeat.

## What goes wrong most

- A middle that is a list: one picture after another with no turn.
- Text too small to read on a phone.
- Too long for the idea.
- A slide-deck layout: the same figure and caption, scene after scene.
- A borrowed look, especially the fly's blueprint plates.
- Music that ignores the picture.

## What the numbers say

Measured on 25 Sep 2026 with `lookcheck.py` and `soundcheck.py`, real time.
The spread is the point. There is no good number, only a number that fits the
intent.

- **Length.** 11 s to 11 min, median 39 s. 16 of 29 run 45 s or less.
- **Pace.** 0 to 2.0 cuts a second, median 0.5. The calm films cut 0.14 to 0.23
  a second and are still loved.
- **Sync.** 10 to 92 percent of picture moments carry a sound, median 56.
  Films whose sounds follow the picture sit at 73 to 92. Films carried by one
  swell sit at 35 to 45, on purpose.
- **Groove.** 32 to 100 percent, median 76. Low means no steady kit, not a
  broken one.
- **Loudness.** -11 to -25 dBFS for good mixes. The one film viewers called far
  too quiet measured -33.
- **Silence.** 2 of 29 have no sound at all.

`verify.py` turns the intent card's pace and sound lines into bands drawn from
these films. A film only has to match what it said it would be.

## Why every-row-twice failed

It passed every check, and Kartik still called it bad. It is the riso flipbook
redrawn: the dot, the bell, the turntable, the ferris wheel, the medallion
ring, a pink second dot, a two-dot end card. It copied one film instead of
learning from many. Its pictures meant nothing about replication. It had no
character. No measurement here can see any of that. Only the intent card and
the watch test can.

## Getting the films

The fxtwitter API reads X without logging in. `https://api.fxtwitter.com/i/status/<id>`
returns a post with its video link. `/2/profile/<handle>/statuses` lists a
maker's recent posts and `/2/conversation/<id>` unrolls a thread, which is how
most of these were found.

```bash
id=2102437977435893771
curl -s "https://api.fxtwitter.com/i/status/$id" \
  | python3 -c "import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)['tweet']['media']['videos'][0]['url'])" \
  | xargs curl -s -L -o film.mp4
```

Download into the session scratchpad, then pull it apart with `references.md`.
Study them and never copy them. Their frames and audio never go into this
skill or into a piece.
