Every time you track a parcel, you only want one answer. Where is it right now? Parcel forty-two just moved house again. In Apache Pinot, the database behind that answer, each house is a row and each street is a segment. Pinot handles this with upserts. It doesn't overwrite the old row, so every house it ever lived in is still standing. So how does your query find only the newest one? It starts with the post: every letter for forty-two comes down this one road, to this one post office. That's the partition, and it means one server sees every update for forty-two. Inside, the postmaster keeps an address book, one line per key. In Pinot, that's the primary key map. Right now, it says forty-two lives on street seven, house twelve, since fourteen oh eight. Then a new letter lands, postmarked at sixteen thirty. It's not older than the book, so he flips two switches. House twelve goes dark, the new house lights up, and the book gets rewritten. Those switches are the valid doc IDs bitmap, with one bit for every row. But suppose a letter shows up late. A snail brings one, postmarked at twelve ten. What do you think happens? It's older than sixteen thirty, so nothing flips. The letter is kept, but its window stays dark. That's why your query, this telescope, only reads the lit windows. And the dark houses? At night, compaction rebuilds the old streets without them. So that's the whole trick. One road, one book, and two switches. Nothing gets overwritten, and for each key, only one window is lit.
