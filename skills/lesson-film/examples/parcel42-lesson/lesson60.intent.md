# Intent

## Judged

takeaway: after one watch, a newcomer can say "Every update for a key lands on the same server. It looks the key up in one in-memory map, and if the new row is not older, it flips two bits: the old row's off and the new row's on. Nothing is overwritten, and queries read only the rows whose bit is on."
feeling: the click of understanding a trick that is simpler than it looked, and warmth for the parcel that went through it.
follow: parcel 42, whose houses are its rows, and the one lit window that is its live row. The kid and the parcel watch from the hill.
point: nothing is ever knocked down. Keeping the newest row visible is two switches and one line in a book, and a late letter changes nothing.
look: sketch C's riso town at dusk going to night, with its post office inside, and sketch A's brass scale for the compare. Riso because Parcel 42's world is riso, and warm windows against a blue dusk make on and off readable at a glance.
text: in-world. Street signs, the book's lines, the postmarks, the switch board's brass plate "validDocIds" and the pencilled bits, written as the voice names them. The full words go to the SRT.
learns from: Parcel 42's day (one character, lived scenes cut against the insides), Typhoon (a gauge for where we are, each real term marked as it is named), How Browsers Work (one object carried through each stage, and its warning that eighteen stages is too many).
ends: a recap that says the takeaway once more on the whole picture, then picture and sound fade off together.
