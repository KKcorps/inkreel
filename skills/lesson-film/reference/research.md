# The research behind the skill

Read this when a rule in `teaching.md`, `voice.md` or `youtube.md` needs its
source, or when someone asks why the skill does something. It holds the
evidence with effect sizes and links. The rules themselves live in the other
files. Research helpers gathered this on 25 Sep 2026. Not every link was opened
by hand, and the two ADHD video studies are the ones the design leans on most.

## Where the skill came from

It fuses two skills. code-film gave the look, the gallery, the taste, one
persistent world, the words written as one paragraph first, the composed tune
and the defect checks. The ToySims visual-explainer and causal-svg skills gave
the teaching: the learner-state walk, one job per beat, a prediction that needs
an inference, evidence before interpretation, recap as retrieval, a fixture of
real values, and grounding every new name at a scene change. The blog-writer
skill later gave the core, which is to track what the viewer knows, asks and
believes.

## Learning from video

- **Off-topic extras hurt.** Seductive details cause small to medium harm
  across 68 studies (Sundararajan and Adesope 2020, meta-analysis). Learners
  with low working memory look at them longer and lose more (Sanchez and Wiley
  2006).
- **Signaling helps.** g = 0.52 for retention and g = 0.31 for transfer
  (Schneider et al. 2018, meta-analysis).
- **Segmenting works even when the video sets the pace.** d = 0.42 retention,
  d = 0.35 transfer (Rey et al. 2019,
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4).
- **Narration beats on-screen text**, d = 0.72, strongest when the system sets
  the pace (Ginns 2005, meta-analysis).
- **Short key terms beat full text on screen.** Full text only helps learners
  with low prior knowledge, system-paced material with no pictures (Adesope
  and Nesbit 2012, 57 studies, https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026147).
- **Conversational style** gives d = 0.30 retention and d = 0.54 transfer, and
  it fades in lessons over 35 minutes (Ginns, Martin and Marsh 2013).
- **Expect small to medium gains.** A 2025 re-analysis of Mayer's lab found
  g = 0.37 overall, shrinking each year (Cromley and Chen 2025).
- **Watch time tops out near six minutes** in 6.9 million edX sessions, and
  drawing held viewers 1.5 to 2 times longer than slides. Correlational, and it
  measures watching, not learning (Guo, Kim and Rubin 2014,
  https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf).
- **Watching a diagram being drawn beats seeing it finished** (Fiorella and
  Mayer 2016, four experiments).
- **Questions bring attention back.** Questions through a 21-minute lecture
  halved mind wandering and tripled note taking (Szpunar et al. 2013,
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1221764110). Answering in your head
  works about as well as aloud (Smith, Roediger and Karpicke 2013, ten
  experiments). Questions before short videos raised scores about five points
  across real classes (ManyClasses 2, Motz et al. 2025,
  https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000978). A prediction helps memory most when the
  answer surprises (Brod 2021, review).
- **Worked examples and faded steps help** in math (g = 0.48, Barbieri et al.
  2023; Atkinson, Renkl and Merrill 2003). Interleaving gives g = 0.42
  (Brunmair and Richter 2019).
- **Fast cutting on exciting content overloads** and lowers recall (Lang et
  al. 1999).

## ADHD and video, directly

- **Zhu et al., ASSETS 2026** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09443). Eye tracking
  with 16 adults with ADHD. Dense text, unexplained visuals, monotone narration
  over static frames and abrupt jumps all hurt. Text that arrived in sync with
  the words helped, and so did animated cues and views that varied but stayed
  connected. Animated cues caught the eye 90% of the time, new text 41%.
- **FocusView 2025** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13309). 9 of 12 ADHD viewers
  turned captions on for every video, and word-by-word highlighting helped
  most. Background music split them. In about 7,000 YouTube and TikTok
  comments, pop-up keywords, animated overlays, background music and sound
  effects were common complaints.
- **Lab findings for adults with ADHD**: moderate working memory deficits
  across 38 studies (Alderson et al. 2013), steeper discounting of delayed
  rewards (d = 0.43, Jackson and MacKillop 2016), more spontaneous mind
  wandering (Seli et al. 2015). White or pink noise helps young people with
  ADHD a little (g = 0.25) and hurts others (g = -0.21) (Nigg's group, JAACAP
  2024). Retrieval practice helps college students with ADHD as much as others
  (Knouse et al. 2016, 2020).
- **Captions for ADHD learning have not been tested properly.** The one study
  is a 2012 pilot with seven people, two with ADHD symptoms (Lewis and Brown,
  https://davidlewisphd.com/publications/2012-AECT-LewisBrown.pdf).

## What the evidence does not support

- An eight-second attention span, or a drop at 10 to 15 minutes. Bradbury
  (2016) found no primary data for either.
- A pattern interrupt every N seconds. That comes from creators reading
  retention graphs, which measure clicking away, not learning.
- More stimulation for ADHD viewers. The one direct study found overload and
  boredom both fail.
- The "interest-based nervous system" is William Dodson's clinical idea and is
  not peer reviewed. Hyperfocus has only been measured by self-report
  (Hupfeld et al. 2019).

## How the channels that hold attention do it

- **Stage the wrong idea first.** Derek Muller's PhD found tidy explanations
  can leave viewers surer of their wrong idea, and videos that staged the
  misconception taught far more
  (https://www.bobvanvliet.com/notes/designing-effective-multimedia-for-physics-education/).
- **Concrete before abstract, and why to care inside 30 seconds.** Grant
  Sanderson (https://www.3blue1brown.com/blog/some1).
- **A new picture about every three seconds.** Kurzgesagt draws about 200
  illustrations for ten minutes, writing is their bottleneck, and each score is
  composed to the finished picture
  (https://10.studio/the-incredible-amount-of-work-behind-kurzgesagts-beautiful-animated-videos/).
  Their openings, pace and endings, measured from caption timings, are in
  `pace-and-ending.md`.
- **One compelling question, walked in order.** Welch Labs runs about 20
  minutes on one question plus history
  (https://aperiodical.com/2025/06/eipi-to-watch-welch-labs/). Animation vs.
  Math holds 14 minutes with no words, carried by a character, a goal and
  escalation.
- **A payoff every few minutes, each opening the next question.** The leaked
  MrBeast guide asks for the first minute to match the title and thumbnail
  (https://www.danielscrivner.com/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production-summary/).
  George Blackman's teaching version is three to four small payoffs in fifteen
  minutes (https://lelon.substack.com/p/everything-ive-learned-about-youtube).
  The shouting surface of that style reads as cheap to a STEM audience.

## YouTube's side

- **Upload spec**: MP4, H.264 High, 4:2:0, a closed GOP of half the frame rate,
  BT.709, AAC-LC 48 kHz (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171).
  Uploads at 1440p or above get VP9 or AV1 at every viewing size in community
  tests (https://filmdaft.com/how-to-get-better-image-quality-on-your-youtube-videos/).
- **Loudness**: YouTube turns anything louder than about -14 LUFS down and
  never turns quiet audio up (https://productionadvice.co.uk/stats-for-nerds/).
- **Chapters** start at 0:00, need three or more, each 10 s or longer
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579). **Captions** from an
  uploaded file beat auto-captions on technical terms
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734698). **Test & Compare** picks
  a thumbnail by watch time (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400).
- **Shorts** run up to three minutes and should link one long video that pays
  them off early (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877,
  https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/).
- **AI narration** is allowed on monetized videos. The July 2025 rule targets
  mass-produced, templated content
  (https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/).

## Voices, licences and timing

- **Kokoro-82M**, Apache 2.0 weights, local, token timestamps
  (https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M).
- **ElevenLabs**, best quality, character timestamps, voice cloning, commercial
  use on paid plans (https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps).
- **Not for published films**: macOS `say` is personal and non-commercial
  (https://www.apple.com/legal/sla/docs/macOSTahoe.pdf), F5-TTS and XTTS-v2
  weights are non-commercial (https://github.com/swivid/f5-tts). Chatterbox is
  MIT and watermarks its output (https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox).
- **Alignment** when a voice has no timestamps: WhisperX with the script as
  text (https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX).
- **Picture to sound**: viewers notice sound that comes early sooner than late.
  ITU-R BT.1359 puts the limits near 45 ms early and 125 ms late.
- **The pattern the voice clock borrows** is manim-voiceover's, where a
  voiceover block sets its own length from its audio and exposes bookmarks as
  times (https://github.com/ManimCommunity/manim-voiceover).

## What turned out different in practice

- The first estimate for capture was about 0.9 s a frame at 1080p. With the
  print finish and cached layers it measured 0.5 to 0.65 s, and 3110 frames took
  about five minutes with 12 Chromes.
- The plan was to cut a long film into chapters with their own worlds. At one
  to two minutes, one page with a scene list and a shared world state was
  enough.
- ElevenLabs and an own-voice recording were planned for publishing. The
  approved film uses Kokoro's `af_heart`, and its one real
  pronunciation problem ("upsert") was fixed by respelling (`voice.md`).
